❓ How to Use

What is SPI?

SPI (Semester Performance Index) is your weighted grade-point average for one semester. GTU works it out as (Credits × Grade Points) added up for every subject, divided by total Credits. It's on a 10-point scale, not a percentage.

Adding a subject

Tap ＋ Add Subject, or pick a Template button that matches your subject's mark scheme (e.g. T+P 200 for a subject with a practical component, Theory 100 for pure theory). Templates fill in the right components with the correct max and pass marks automatically, so you don't have to look them up.

Components

Each subject is built from components — GTU ESE (the university exam), Mid Exam, Practical Internal, and so on. Enter marks directly, or switch a component to Grade mode if you only know your letter grade for it.

The 🎯 exam star

Mark one component as the exam component — usually the GTU final. Leave its marks blank, and the calculator tells you exactly what score you'd need there to hit your Target SPI, plus what SPI each possible grade would give you.

Target SPI slider

Drag the slider near the top to set your goal (5.0 – 10.0). Every prediction on the page updates live against that number.

Credits & audit subjects

Double-check the credit value for each subject — it's what the SPI formula weights by. If a subject is an audit course that shouldn't count toward SPI, set Include in SPI to No.

Failing a subject (FF)

If you miss a pass mark, the subject is auto-graded FF (0 points) — but its credits still count in the SPI total, per GTU rules. That's why an FF pulls your SPI down rather than just being ignored.

Saving your work

Everything auto-saves in this browser as you type. Use 💾 Saves to name and keep multiple snapshots — different semesters, or "what-if" scenarios — and reload any of them later. Use 📤 Share to send a quick text summary of your marks to WhatsApp or anywhere else.

Nothing you enter leaves this device — all calculation happens locally in your browser.
